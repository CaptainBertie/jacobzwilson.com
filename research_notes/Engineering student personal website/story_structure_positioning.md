# Story, Structure and Positioning of a Personal Website for an Engineering Student (Sydney, 2026)

Method note: searches returned mostly secondary/practitioner sources; NN/g primary pages were fetched where possible. No rigorous hiring data specific to Australian engineering graduates was found. Two fetches (The Muse hiring-managers piece, Ask a Manager) returned 403, so those are cited only from search-snippet summaries.

## 1. What the first screen should communicate; how visitors scan homepages and About pages

### Takeaway
The first screen should say who you are, what you do/are aiming at, and where to go next, in plain words. NN/g evidence says people scan, not read, so key facts go first, in the top-left, in information-carrying words.

### Cited Findings
- NN/g eyetracking (232 users, thousands of pages) found an F-shaped scan: a read across the top, a shorter second sweep lower, then a vertical scan down the left edge; "users won't read your text thoroughly." Put key information in the first two paragraphs and start headings/bullets with information-carrying words. It is a rough tendency (can look like E or inverted L), not a rule. — [NN/g F-pattern](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content-discovered/)
- NN/g says the F-pattern persists on desktop and mobile; fixes are standout headings, bullets, bolded key phrases. — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/F-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/) (via search summary)
- NN/g About Us research (3 rounds over 10+ years, 70+ users, 100 sites tested): users want clear plain-language explanations of what you do, authenticity (real photos, human copy), and easy-to-find contact options. Hurt by stock photos, dense text, clutter, weak contrast, poor mobile layout. Skepticism has risen. Many users skip About pages and just search ("I'd just Google it"). Job seekers researching an employer are a listed visitor type (company context, not individuals). — [NN/g About Us](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/about-us-information-on-websites/)
- NN/g recommends About information at four levels: homepage tagline, About page summary, subpages, footer links; and explicit link labels because users rarely click obscure ones. — [NN/g About Us](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/about-us-information-on-websites/)
- An archived US federal usability guide (NN/g-era research) found a tagline increases users' understanding of a site; homepages should state purpose prominently. — [usability.gov ch.5 archive](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20080916111221/http:/usability.gov/pdfs/chapter5.pdf) (via search summary)
- Reviewer time is short: MIT's engineering communication guide says portfolio reviewers may skim each page for 30 seconds to a minute. — [MIT CommLab](https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/meche/commkit/portfolio/)
- Blog-level claims (weak sources): first impression forms in ~10 s, then 2-5 minutes on projects; "80% of recruiters spend 3 minutes or less" is from a design-recruiting context. — [Fueler](https://www.fueler.io/blog/how-recruiters-evaluate-your-digital-portfolio), [UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/ever-wonder-how-recruiters-look-at-your-design-portfolio-cc8dd1ecb698)
- Secondary summaries attributing "median 11.4 s on a homepage" and "5-second test, 80% recall" to NN/g could not be verified. — [Portent](https://portent.com/blog/content/tips-to-improve-your-homepage-ux.htm)

### Inferences
- NN/g's About research is about organisations, not individuals; applying it to a person is an extrapolation. The transferable bits: plain language, real photo, visible contact, honest tone.
- A student's homepage should work in about 10 seconds: name, one-line positioning, 2-3 strongest projects, a contact/CV link, all visible without scrolling on a phone.

### Gaps
- No NN/g study specific to personal/portfolio sites or recruiter eyetracking of them.
- Timing figures (10 s, 3 min) are unverified practitioner claims.

## 2. Standard vs optional pages; blog/writing evidence

### Takeaway
Core set is home, projects, short about, CV/resume (PDF), contact. Blog and "now" page are optional; a stale blog is worse than none.

### Cited Findings
- One engineer-focused guide: landing page (name, short intro, what you work on); About (brief, specific bio); 3-6 substantive projects; writing/talks (few strong, "3 strong posts beat 30 weak ones"); link to current PDF resume; contact by email and LinkedIn, no web forms. Avoid templates, heavy animation, mission statements, long tech lists, self-rated skill bars, stale content. — [techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474627/personal-website-portfolio-engineers/)
- Same source: build a site if frontend/design/product-leaning or with substantial public work; backend/infra/data engineers' sites are rarely clicked through, and GitHub/LinkedIn cover the same ground; skip if there is no public work to link (reads performative); 4-8 hours is typical, 40+ is over-engineering; review quarterly; "an outdated site is worse than none." — [techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474627/personal-website-portfolio-engineers/) (single practitioner source, not research)
- Blog: often abandoned within a year; commit to monthly or skip; a "Selected writing" of 3-5 posts suffices. — [techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474627/personal-website-portfolio-engineers/)
- Another source says do not expect inbound recruiter contact from a portfolio site; treat it as "resume++" with the resume first. — search summary of [techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474627/personal-website-portfolio-engineers/)
- UC Irvine engineering careers: always include your resume on the site. — [UCI](https://undergraduate.eng.uci.edu/students/career-prep/engineering-portfolio)
- Randstad Australia (mechanical/civil/electrical focus): pick 2-3 strong projects; use a simple hub (Notion/Wix) tying models to your career story; link the portfolio from LinkedIn "Featured"; show decision-making, not just tool lists; embed CAD/HMI demos. — [Randstad AU](https://www.randstad.com.au/career-advice/career-tips/online-portfolio-engineering-jobs/)
- MIT: 3-4 main projects, keep a master portfolio and tailor a subset per recipient; web portfolios should be sortable by skill/topic; avoid common class projects reviewers have seen many times. — [MIT CommLab](https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/meche/commkit/portfolio/)
- "Now" page: Derek Sivers' /now page shares "what you'd tell a friend you hadn't seen in a year"; meant for people who already have an About page; personal, not marketing. — [nownownow.com/about](https://nownownow.com/about)
- Opinion split on student sites: some hiring managers say they hired because of a great site; a widely read hiring blog calls university-mandated student sites "terrible, cheesy, unnecessary". "56% of hiring managers impressed" traces to a vendor survey (Workfolio) and should be treated cautiously. — [The Muse](https://www.themuse.com/advice/can-a-personal-website-help-your-job-search-what-6-hiring-managers-really-think), [Ask a Manager](https://askamanager.org/2016/09/my-university-requires-students-to-create-terrible-personal-websites-for-job-searching.html) (both via search summary; 403 on fetch)

### Inferences
- For hardware + embedded + trading-systems work, the site probably earns its keep because project evidence (photos, schematics, results) does not fit a resume or GitHub README. The "skip it" advice applies mainly to pure backend/infra people.
- A "Now" page doubles as a freshness signal and a low-effort place for sport/interests without cluttering the homepage, but it is a maintenance liability if not updated.
- Blog recommendation: no blog at start; at most a small "Notes" list of 2-3 real write-ups (e.g. a post-mortem on a trading system) that double as project evidence.

### Gaps
- No controlled evidence on whether personal sites raise interview rates for graduates; Australian graduate-program recruiter commentary on personal sites not found.

## 3. Writing a credible About / personal narrative for a student

### Takeaway
Short, specific, first-person, evidence-led; concrete projects beat adjectives; a personal detail and a call to action close it.

### Cited Findings
- UGA Career Center six-step About guide: basics (name, field, focus); strengths and passions tied to coursework/campus/internships; a bit of personality (hobby/fun fact); easy-to-connect call to action with contact; friendly photo; refresh each semester. Tone "genuine and concise." — [UGA](https://career.uga.edu/portfolios_about_me)
- General bio guidance: 3-5 sentences for a resume "About"; first person is more approachable; consider who reads it; include role-relevant keywords; lead with a quantified result where possible. — search summaries of [Cake](https://www.cake.me/resources/resume/about-me-on-resume), [Codecademy](https://www.codecademy.com/resources/blog/how-to-write-a-programmer-bio-examples)
- Recruiters do not read mission statements; replace with specific work descriptions. — [techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474627/personal-website-portfolio-engineers/)
- Randstad AU: recruiters want evidence of decision-making, which lowers perceived hiring risk. — [Randstad AU](https://www.randstad.com.au/career-advice/career-tips/online-portfolio-engineering-jobs/)
- NN/g: users are more skeptical than before; authenticity shown by real photos and human copy. — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/about-us-information-on-websites/)

### Inferences
- A present / past / future frame (what you build now; the 1-2 experiences that led here; what you want next) is the author's suggestion, not stated in any source found. It suits a student because "present" can be projects and coursework, not jobs.
- Clichés to avoid (from the sources' anti-patterns plus inference): mission statements, "passionate about", skill bars, stock photos, lists of every technology.
- No source found offered a named storytelling framework from Australian careers services.

### Gaps
- No UTS/UNSW/USYD/Monash page found specifically on writing an About page; Monash returned only generic employability hubs.
- Storytelling frameworks (e.g. STAR is for interviews, not About pages) not verified as recommended for About sections.

## 4. Generalist vs specialist; one target vs several audiences

### Takeaway
Sources favour "T-shaped" framing (one deeper area plus breadth), but evidence is practitioner opinion. Tailoring comes from selecting and ordering projects per audience, not building several sites.

### Cited Findings
- Practitioner consensus: generalist vs specialist is a false dichotomy; aim for T-shaped; pi-shaped means two deep areas. — [TechTarget](https://techtarget.com/whatis/feature/Generalist-vs-specialist-Whats-the-difference), [DEV](https://dev.to/alexkondov/the-t-shaped-engineer-3io3)
- Counterpoint: a T takes time to build and is "a great end goal, but not a reasonable early career strategy." — [Work Smarter](https://worksmarter.beehiiv.com/p/depth-vs-breadth)
- GradConnection: you may start generalist or specialist and grow vertically or horizontally later. — [GradConnection](https://nz.gradconnection.com/career-advice/article/generalist-vs-specialist-is-one-better-than-the-other/)
- MIT: choose projects matching the role's most valuable skills; keep a master set and send a tailored subset; make web portfolios sortable by skill/topic. — [MIT CommLab](https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/meche/commkit/portfolio/)
- USYD careers: LinkedIn headline should point at a target career. — search summary of [USYD](https://sydney.edu.au/careers/students/finding-jobs/how-to-find-a-job/social-media-and-your-job-search.html)
- Techinterview: leave non-engineering content off unless it shows relevant skills. — [techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474627/personal-website-portfolio-engineers/)

### Inferences
- Coherence device: one connecting thread (e.g. "builds systems that sense, decide and act, from firmware to trading logic") with each interest as a project under it, rather than separate silos. This is the author's synthesis, not a sourced recommendation.
- Marketing coursework is a differentiator only if tied to engineering (e.g. product thinking, communication); otherwise keep it to the CV.
- Sport: one line in About or on /now (shows teamwork/discipline), not a section.
- Different audiences (hardware vs software roles) can be served by project tags/filters and a per-application CV, with a single primary headline.
- Trading-systems projects: consider employer sensitivity; not covered by sources.

### Gaps
- No hiring-manager data on how graduate recruiters read multi-disciplinary candidates; claimed salary premiums and "Google found 70% T-shaped" were unsourced and excluded.

## 5. Student-specific content: coursework, teams, hackathons, grades, evolution over time

### Takeaway
Show real artefacts from coursework, teams and personal builds with your own role stated; list WAM only if strong and label it; update the site each semester.

### Cited Findings
- Westpac graduate guide (via Prosple): listing GPA/WAM is optional because employers usually request a transcript at application. — [Prosple/Westpac](https://prod-1621.frontend.prosple.com/applying/westpac-student-application-survival-guide-resume-writing)
- US advice commonly sets 3.5+ as a threshold; engineering named as a field where GPA is expected; a stronger major-GPA can be listed too (US-scale, commercial resume blogs). — [Monster](https://www.monster.com/career-advice/article/how-to-include-gpa), [Forage](https://www.theforage.com/blog/basics/gpa-on-resume)
- MIT warns against common class projects everyone has seen. — [MIT CommLab](https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/meche/commkit/portfolio/)
- UGA: update the About page each semester or after internships/study abroad/campus involvement; USYD says extracurriculars can be listed on LinkedIn from first year. — [UGA](https://career.uga.edu/portfolios_about_me), [USYD](https://sydney.edu.au/campus-life/student-news/2017/07/19/5-tips-for-making-the-most-of-linkedin.html)
- Techinterview: if you won't maintain it quarterly, skip; no public work means reads performative. — [techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474627/personal-website-portfolio-engineers/)

### Inferences
- First-year: 1-2 projects + About + contact; second/third year: add team projects with role clearly stated; graduation year: add CV, outcomes, internships, and a "seeking" line with dates. Stage plan is the author's inference.
- WAM: given Westpac's "optional", put it on the CV and only on the site if it is a strength; state it as WAM with scale (e.g. "WAM 7x / 100") since international readers read GPA. This is the author's judgment.

### Gaps
- No Australian source with WAM thresholds for graduate programs; no source on hackathon credibility or how to present group work specifically; no UNSW/UTS/USYD/Monash official portfolio guidance found (UNSW pages found describe UNSWConnect and employability, not websites).

## 6. Calls to action, contact, privacy, domain and email

### Takeaway
Make email, LinkedIn and CV one click away on every page; keep home address, phone and WHOIS data private; use a first-last name domain and matching email.

### Cited Findings
- Contact: email and LinkedIn at minimum, no web forms (single source). — [techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474627/personal-website-portfolio-engineers/)
- UGA: end About with a call to action (view work, connect, message) and include email and phone; NN/g: easy-to-find contact options are a core user want. — [UGA](https://career.uga.edu/portfolios_about_me), [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/about-us-information-on-websites/)
- Privacy: keep home address, personal phone and personal email off public pages; avoid family names, school, routines; strip photo location data; WHOIS records list name/address/phone/email unless domain privacy is enabled (many registrars now default it); data-broker listings are another exposure. — [PEN America manual](https://onlineharassmentfieldmanual.pen.org/?p=333), [Kaspersky](https://www.kaspersky.com/blog/doxing-methods/39651/), [it.com](https://get.it.com/blog/doxxing-in-the-domain-industry-what-it-is-and-how-to-protect-yourself/) (via search summary; no student-specific source)
- Domain: custom domain about $12/year reads more professional; favour .dev or similar, avoid novelty TLDs. — [techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474627/personal-website-portfolio-engineers/)
- Email: first-and-last-name format is the safe default; avoid nicknames, numbers, underscores; university addresses can be deleted after graduation (advice varies, Ohio State says university address is fine); same address across resume and profiles; custom-domain email is an upgrade. — [Enhancv](https://enhancv.com/blog/how-do-you-choose-a-professional-email-address-for-a-resume), [Hostinger](https://www.hostinger.com/au/tutorials/?p=144442), [Naukri](https://naukri.com/campus/career-guidance/professional-email-address-on-resume) (commercial/blog sources)
- Randstad AU: before posting client/project work, check confidentiality terms was not addressed by the source (reader inference only); check employer terms for internship work.

### Inferences
- For a Sydney student: a recruiter needs only email, LinkedIn and a CV link; city/suburb-level location ("Sydney, NSW") and work-rights status (a line recruiters in AU look for) are enough, no phone or address on the public page, phone only on the downloadable CV if at all. Work-rights line is the author's inference and not sourced.
- Contact-form avoidance (single source) is partly a spam/maintenance trade-off; email obfuscation was not researched.

### Gaps
- No Australian source on publishing phone numbers/addresses on CVs; no source on .au domain choice (.com.au/.id.au eligibility) was researched.
- No source on recruiters' actions after landing on a site (e.g. whether a CV download or "Open to internships" banner increases responses).

## 7. Consistency across site, LinkedIn, GitHub and resume

### Takeaway
Use the same name, photo, headline and project list everywhere, and link them to each other; sources agree on the principle but offer little rigorous evidence.

### Cited Findings
- USYD: headline should point to a target career with university named; start the profile in first year. UTS: keep profiles current, use a professional photo, avoid extreme views, use privacy settings. — [USYD](https://sydney.edu.au/careers/students/finding-jobs/how-to-find-a-job/social-media-and-your-job-search.html), [UTS](https://www.uts.edu.au/news/2018/11/students-developing-personal-brand-online-right-way)
- Student LinkedIn guides: headline/summary should name year, major, skills and projects rather than "student at X" (blog sources). — [Michael Page](https://www.michaelpage.be/advice/career-advice/linkedin-tips-for-students)
- Randstad AU: link the portfolio from LinkedIn "Featured." — [Randstad AU](https://www.randstad.com.au/career-advice/career-tips/online-portfolio-engineers/)
- Hostinger: same email address on resume, LinkedIn and other profiles. — [Hostinger](https://www.hostinger.com/au/tutorials/?p=144442)

### Inferences
- One master "positioning line" reused as site headline, LinkedIn headline, GitHub bio and CV summary; GitHub pinned repos should match the site's featured projects.
- Name consistency (same spelling, same handle across domain/GitHub/LinkedIn) is the author's inference.

### Gaps
- No empirical study on cross-platform consistency effects; Monash and UNSW-specific LinkedIn guidance not found.

## Source quality summary
- Strong: NN/g (F-pattern, About Us), MIT CommLab, UGA Career Center, USYD/UTS pages (older, 2017-2018).
- Medium: Randstad AU, Prosple/Westpac guide.
- Weak or commercial: techinterview.org (single practitioner blog, but the most engineer-specific), resume-builder and hosting blogs, vendor surveys (Workfolio), SEO aggregators.
