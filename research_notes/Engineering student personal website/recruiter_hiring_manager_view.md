# Recruiter and hiring-manager view of engineering student personal websites (2026, Australia focus)

Overall evidence quality: weak. No rigorous study measures how often or how long recruiters open an engineering graduate's personal website. Most of what exists is small informal surveys, single hiring-manager posts, recruiter-firm marketing, and unsourced blogs. Items are tagged [HARD], [NAMED/ANECDOTE] or [OPINION].

## Do recruiters and hiring managers open personal websites, at what stage, for how long?

### Takeaway
Hiring managers who are the technical reviewer probably do open links if they are clean and quick to scan, but there is no hard data on rates or dwell time. The only well-known timing figure (about 7 seconds) is for resumes, not websites, and comes from a thinly documented vendor study.

### Cited Findings
- [HARD-ish, dated, 2021] Informal survey by Johannes Kettmann (Profy.dev) of "300+ recruiters and React team leads", about 60 responses via Google Forms, rated two scenarios 0-5. An "overwhelming majority" said they would look at a junior's website; the most common answer to "would the candidate without a site have lower chances" was "It depends". No percentages, no response rate, no time-on-site measured. Frontend/React-specific, self-selected. — [DEV Community article, Aug 2021](https://dev.to/profydev/this-survey-among-60-hiring-managers-reveals-don-t-waste-your-time-on-a-react-portfolio-website-17ge)
- [HARD, dated 2018, vendor] Ladders eye-tracking study: initial resume screen about 7.4 seconds (up from about 6 in 2012). Sample size, role types and resume lengths not disclosed; Ladders is a commercial job site; critics call methodology thin. This is resume time only, not website time. — [HR Dive, Nov 2018](https://www.hrdive.com/news/eye-tracking-study-shows-recruiters-look-at-resumes-for-7-seconds/541582/); critique: [ERE](https://www.ere.net/articles/is-the-6-second-resume-scan-a-myth)
- Resume layout findings from the same study: simple layouts with clear headings, bold titles and bulleted accomplishments held attention; clutter, multiple columns, long sentences hurt. — [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/eye-tracking-study-shows-recruiters-look-at-resumes-for-7-seconds/541582/)
- [NAMED/ANECDOTE] Matt, hiring manager for mechanical engineers at Trossen Robotics (Jan 2025): opens the portfolio first to judge project fit, then the resume. Applications without a portfolio are deleted (about 50% of applicants send none). — [Trossen Robotics](https://www.trossenrobotics.com/post/how-not-to-get-a-job-as-a-mechanical-engineer)
- [OPINION, unsourced] "30 seconds to 2 minutes" on a portfolio and "6 minutes vs 45 seconds" (attributed to a "2025 NACE study") circulate in blogs; I could not verify either and the NACE attribution looks unreliable. Do not use. — search results via blogs; unverified
- Unverified figures also circulating: 15 seconds vs about 3 minutes per portfolio, "73% Stack Overflow 2024". Could not trace to primary sources. — unverified

### Inferences
- A safe working assumption is a 30-60 second scan by whoever opens it, so the first screen has to carry the pitch. This is an inference, not a measured figure.
- Stage: the Trossen example is resume/portfolio screen; most other guidance implies the link is checked at resume screen or just before interview. No source gives stage data.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or large-n recruiter survey on personal-website open rates for engineering grads.
- No eye-tracking study of portfolio sites (Ladders covered resumes only).
- Profy.dev's named quotes are images; their text was not extractable.

## What they look for first, and turn-offs

### Takeaway
Reviewers want evidence of real, original, working projects explained with context and outcomes, reachable in one or two clicks. Common turn-offs: tutorial clones, broken or stale content, flashy navigation that buries projects, and no explanation.

### Cited Findings
- [NAMED/ANECDOTE] Trossen's Matt: wants lots of images of projects plus short text; complex multi-part designs (simple brackets and bolts "will not land an interview"); a working link is essential; flashy, hard-to-navigate sites that bury projects hurt; a portfolio "is not a place to showcase your web dev skills"; Google Drive folders of CAD files are worse since "No manager is going to download your files and load them into a CAD program"; leave out hobbies unrelated to mechanical skills. Prefers a printable PDF. — [Trossen Robotics](https://www.trossenrobotics.com/post/how-not-to-get-a-job-as-a-mechanical-engineer)
- Profy.dev survey cited red flags: poor design reads as less competent; broken or outdated content from neglected maintenance; a commenter reports rejecting candidates for overengineered sites (anecdote in comments). Positives: shows character, dedication, especially for juniors; GitHub can show the same. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/profydev/this-survey-among-60-hiring-managers-reveals-don-t-waste-your-time-on-a-react-portfolio-website-17ge)
- [OPINION, editorial] Dice (May 2025, unnamed staff): depth beats volume; red flags are many incomplete or superficial projects, repos with no structure or documentation, irregular commit history, duty-only resume bullets, "code ninja/rockstar". — [Dice](https://www.dice.com/career-advice/your-first-resume-and-portfolio-what-tech-hiring-managers-really-want-to-see)
- [OPINION, unsourced] techinterview.org lists red flags: bootcamp templates, heavy animation/parallax/hero video, long mission statements, skill bars, stale blog posts, "Coming Soon" sections, joke TLDs. The page cites no data. — [techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474627/personal-website-portfolio-engineers/)
- [OPINION] Multiple SEO blogs say tutorial clones (Netflix/Spotify/Twitter) read as "tutorial follower", and original problem-solving projects or one merged open-source PR beat several solo clones. Consistent but none is hard evidence. — [Substack roundup](https://arslandg.substack.com/p/3-portfolio-projects-that-actually)

### Inferences
- Consensus across sources, though all soft: clarity of the first screen, finished projects with outcomes, working links, and no maintenance rot. Slow-site complaints appear in advice blogs but I found no named recruiter citing page speed.

### Gaps
- No hard data on the relative weight of each turn-off.
- Did not find the Ask HN hiring-manager comments (page title only); no verified Reddit hiring-manager quotes on personal websites. A Reddit MechE hiring-manager ATS post (r/EngineeringResumes, Jan 2024) exists but returned 403, so unread.

## Software vs hardware / mechanical / mechatronics / electrical

### Takeaway
For software, GitHub and the resume carry most weight and a personal site is optional; for mechanical, design and robotics work, visual portfolios (images, CAD renders, test results) matter more because the work cannot be read as code. The mechanical evidence is a single named manager plus recruiter marketing.

### Cited Findings
- [OPINION, unsourced] Frontend/design recruiters click through "frequently"; backend/infra/data rarely, where LinkedIn and GitHub "cover the same ground". Hardware not addressed. — [techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474627/personal-website-portfolio-engineers/)
- [NAMED/ANECDOTE] Mechanical: Trossen requires a portfolio for MechE, but prefers a simple one, ideally a PDF; discourages a showy personal website. — [Trossen Robotics](https://www.trossenrobotics.com/post/how-not-to-get-a-job-as-a-mechanical-engineer)
- [OPINION, vendor] Portfolios are most recommended for design-oriented roles and specialties like robotics and automation; interns are not expected to have polished personal projects, internship hiring is a bet on potential (one commenter who hires interns). — search summary of mechanical engineering guides; commenter source not directly verified
- [OPINION, vendor, 5 May 2026] Randstad Australia: PDFs "cannot capture 3D logic, BIM complexity, or SCADA flows"; recommends 2-3 projects, interactive 3D models (glTF/OBJ, Sketchfab), a clickable HMI/SCADA simulation for electrical/systems roles, a hub page (Notion, Wix, GitHub Pages) and a LinkedIn Featured link. Cites no data; commercial interest; claim that portfolio candidates are "fast-tracked by clients" is asserted not shown. — [Randstad Australia](https://www.randstad.com.au/career-advice/career-tips/online-portfolio-engineering-jobs/)

### Inferences
- A PDF portfolio plus a lightweight web page hosting the same material covers both Trossen-style and web-first reviewers. Inference.
- Electrical/embedded evidence is thin: no source found specific to EE hiring managers; schematics, PCB photos and demo video are the natural equivalents (inference).

### Gaps
- No hardware-specific survey; electrical/mechatronics hiring-manager statements not found.
- NDA and confidentiality limits on publishing internship work are unaddressed by sources.

## Website vs resume, LinkedIn, GitHub

### Takeaway
Sources agree the resume gets you screened, LinkedIn is where Australian recruiters search, GitHub is verifiable proof for code, and a website's unique job is presenting non-code work with context in one place. None of this is measured.

### Cited Findings
- Resume: 7.4 s initial scan (2018 vendor study); the resume is the gate. — [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/eye-tracking-study-shows-recruiters-look-at-resumes-for-7-seconds/541582/)
- [OPINION] Randstad AU: share the portfolio link in LinkedIn Featured because Australian recruiters are active there. — [Randstad Australia](https://www.randstad.com.au/career-advice/career-tips/online-portfolio-engineering-jobs/)
- [OPINION] A 2025 blog guide for Australian graduate engineers says LinkedIn is where recruiters go to find and vet talent. — [Engineers Connect AU blog](https://engineersconnectau.blogspot.com/2025/04/how-to-stand-out-as-graduate-engineer.html)
- [NAMED/ANECDOTE] Alumna quoted on UTS pages recommends LinkedIn and GitHub to show what you have built; her posts led to a research invitation from UTS lecturers. — [UTS Experience](https://www.uts.edu.au/for-students/experience-uts/postgraduate/five-tips-to-land-a-job-before-you-graduate)
- [OPINION] For roles whose work sits in private corporate repos, LinkedIn plus GitHub already do the public-facing job and a personal site adds little. — techinterview.org (unsourced), above
- Hiring-manager statements found via search (not individually verified): "if I can't click something, it barely exists"; another manager reads personal sites and repos and asks about them in interviews. — search summary, see Hackernoon/Holloway results; verify before quoting

### Inferences
- Division of labour (inference): resume = gate and keywords for ATS; LinkedIn = discoverability and credibility; GitHub = code evidence; website = narrative and visuals for non-code work, and the one place you control. Trossen shows a PDF can substitute for the website in hardware.

### Gaps
- No study comparing outcomes for candidates with and without a website. The Profy.dev survey is the only direct proxy and found the absence does not clearly hurt ("It depends").

## Australia-specific evidence

### Takeaway
I found no Australian survey, Engineers Australia statement, GradConnection/Prosple report, or UTS Careers guidance addressing recruiters opening personal websites. Australian evidence is limited to AAGE employer/candidate survey data on AI and general UTS and recruiter-firm advice.

### Cited Findings
- 2024 AAGE Employer Survey (via HireVue): only 16% of Australian organisations used AI in recruitment, 37% of those for screening video interviews. Dated 2024. — [HireVue summary](https://www.hirevue.com/blog/hiring/global-graduate-hiring-trends-report)
- 2026 AAGE Candidate Survey: 42% of graduates use AI to support applications, up from 27% the year before. — [AHRI](https://www.ahri.com.au/articles/graduate-hiring-remains-stable-despite-ai-disruption-research-finds)
- Australian graduate market stabilised in early 2026 with postings above pre-pandemic levels (AHRI); Indeed Hiring Lab, April 2026, also reports Australian graduates still being hired. — [AHRI](https://www.ahri.com.au/articles/graduate-hiring-remains-stable-despite-ai-disruption-research-finds); [Indeed Hiring Lab](https://www.hiringlab.org/au/blog/2026/04/22/nice-try-ai-australian-graduates-are-still-getting-hired/)
- NACE (US, not Australia): about 70% of employers report skills-based hiring in 2026 outlook, up from 65%; nothing on portfolios. — [NACE](https://naceweb.org/job-market/trends-and-predictions/almost-two-thirds-of-employers-use-skills-based-hiring-to-help-identify-job-candidates)
- UTS Careers: services open to students and graduates within 2 years; its blog calls an online showcase important for creative fields and LinkedIn management a "MUST". — [UTS Careers Blog](https://careersblog.uts.edu.au/job-search-undergraduate/)
- GradConnection is now SEEK Grad; listing page content did not address portfolios. — [SEEK Grad](https://au.gradconnection.com/graduate-jobs/engineering/melbourne/)

### Inferences
- Large Australian graduate programs (Arup, Aurecon, CIMIC, Ausenco, AECOM appear on university job boards) typically run online applications with structured selection, so a website would be a secondary supplement, not part of the process. This is an inference; no source states it.

### Gaps
- Nothing from Engineers Australia, Prosple, GradConnection or UTS Careers on portfolios/websites for engineers. Best next step: ask UTS Careers or UTS Engineering industry liaison directly.

## AI screening and AI-generated portfolios (2025-2026)

### Takeaway
Hirers increasingly assume polished portfolios might be AI-made and verify by asking candidates to explain decisions. This favours sites with process evidence, real photos, dated artefacts and consistency with LinkedIn/GitHub.

### Cited Findings
- 2025 survey of 874 HR professionals (vendor-reported): 51% had seen AI-generated portfolios among fraudulent submissions, 42% fabricated references, 39% counterfeit diplomas. Gartner's "1 in 4 candidate profiles fake by 2028" is secondhand. — [HCAmag AU](https://www.hcamag.com/au/news/general/candidate-fraud-the-warning-signs-hr-should-watch-out-for/573881)
- Warning signs: GitHub account created a month before applying, cloned repos, shallow derivative work; advice to cross-check LinkedIn, GitHub and portfolio, and to use live explanation or work samples; AI detectors not reliable. Vendor-heavy sources. — [HCAmag](https://www.hcamag.com/au/news/general/candidate-fraud-the-warning-signs-hr-should-watch-out-for/573881); [Fueler](https://fueler.io/blog/how-to-tell-if-a-portfolio-was-made-with-ai)
- Many recruiters use AI-assisted resume screening; a claimed 2025 InterviewPal figure of 11.2 s initial scan with AI tools is unverified. — [Interview Guys](https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/?p=8856)
- University of Melbourne-linked reporting flags discrimination risk in Australian AI recruitment; no specific Australian disclosure law. — [SBS](https://www.sbs.com.au/news/article/algorithmic-discrimination-how-the-use-of-ai-in-job-hiring-may-disadvantage-some-people/t32y910ch)

### Inferences
- A site's value as a trust signal rises when it contains evidence that is hard to fake: photos of physical builds, test data, dated version history, a short "what I decided and why" per project. Inference from the verification advice.
- Plain, static, fast sites are more machine-readable by AI screeners; no source tests this.

### Gaps
- No study of how AI screeners ingest personal websites.
- No 2026 recruiter-trust study specific to portfolios.

## Source-quality notes
- Strongest named engineering-hiring evidence: Trossen Robotics (one MechE manager, Jan 2025).
- Pre-2022 and possibly dated: Profy.dev survey (2021, React-focused), Ladders (2018).
- Treat as marketing: Randstad AU (2026), techinterview.org, Dice (unnamed), fraud-detection vendor blogs.
